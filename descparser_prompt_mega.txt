## Context: Serials Enumeration and Chronology  
  
Serials are continuing resources that are published without a predetermined end date and typically on a regular schedule. Each issue is distinguished from the others by some form of enumeration designation, chronological designation, or both. These designations are required to positively identify specific issues of a serial, but their form can vary widely between different publications. When presented with the unstructured description for a serial, you must first determine what data pertain to enumeration, what data pertain to chronology, and whether any data are superfluous.  
  
### Enumeration  
  
Enumeration refers to the sequential designation of issues of a serial. Enumerations frequently contain a hierarchy of designations, where multiple instances of the lower level must occur before the higher level iterates. The most common form for enumeration data is a combination of volume (the top level enumeration) and issue numbers (the lower level). For example, a journal published quarterly may have one volume per year, with each volume containing four issues (vol. 1, no. 1, 2, 3, and 4, followed by vol. 2, no. 1, etc.). In theory, there may be up to four levels of enumeration data, though items with more than three levels of enumeration (typically volume, issue, and part) are exceedingly rare.  
  
When parsing enumeration data, it is important to identify the levels of enumeration present. Regardless  of the specific designation of the enumeration data, the highest level enumeration must always be associated with the Enum A key, with subsequent levels following on alphabetically.  
  
### Chronology  
  
Chronology refers to the publication time of the issue. As with enumeration, multiple levels are possible. The highest level of chronology will always be the year, expressed as a four digit number. The second and subsequent levels may refer to the season, the month, the week, or even the day of publication. In theory, there may be up to three levels of chronology in a given designation, but in practice, designations with more than two levels of chronology (typically year and season or month) are exceedingly rare. Chronology fields may include date ranges (signified by dashes, like 1915-1918) or multiple dates (signified by slashes, like March/April). The punctuation between dates should be preserved when multiple dates are present.  
  
## Rules  
  
You will be given an unstructured description for an issue of a serial. It is your task to parse the description, identify the enumeration and chronology data, associate them with the most suitable level of description, and return a valid Python dictionary including the data. You must follow the rules below while completing this task:  
  
1. Enum A will correspond with the higher level of enumeration for the resource, usually the volume number (though the exact designation may vary). Possible variants include equivalent designations from other languages (Heft/Band, Liber, etc.) or sequential designations based on year (anno, Jahrgang, etc.). Transcribe **only** the numerical data for Enum A, omitting any prefixes or suffixes (omitting, for example, "vol. " from "vol. 6" and "nd" from "2nd"). Data may include ranges of numbers (e.g., 4-6) which should retain the punctuation when transcribed.  
2. Enum B will correspond with the lower level of enumeration, usually the issue number, but may also be designated as "part" (again, the exact designation may vary). When only one level of enumeration is present, prefer Enum A. Transcribe **only** the numerical data for Enum B, omitting any prefixes or suffixes. Data may include ranges of numbers (e.g., 1-4) which should retain the punctuation when transcribed.  
3. If subsequent levels of enumeration are present, they should be parsed into fields Enum C and Enum D (in that order). Remember to prefer higher levels of enumeration where present and never skip a level when filling in data.  
4. Chron I will correspond to the year. This will always include at least a four-digit number between 1850 and 2050. It may additionally include a range of dates or multiple dates (e.g., 2024/25 or 1956-1958). If there is a range of numbers, they will always be in ascending order (e.g., 1925/26 which refers to 1925 and 1926 or 2014-2021). These should be transcribed exactly, including any punctuation.  
5. Chron J will correspond to the lower level of chronology. This could include months or seasons. Lower-level chronology data should be transcribed exactly.  
6. If a subsequent level of chronological designation exists, it should be parsed into the field Chron K.  
7. Any keys without data present in the description should be returned with empty strings as the associated value.
8. The dataset may include parliamentary papers that will use the parliament (parl.) and session (sess.) instead of typical enumeration. If present, these should be copied into Enum A and Enum B respectively.  
9. Supplements (often tagged suppl.) are extra issues associated with particular volumes. The text 'supplement' or 'suppl.' should be included in the next available enumeration field when present in the description. Similarly, indexes or indices may be marked as such, and this information should be included in the next available enumeration field.  
10. Some serials may use pagination instead of issue numbers. If present, pagination (pp.) should be included in Enum B. Remember not to transcribe prefixes/abbreviations for pagination or other enumeration designations.  
11. Some inputs may include multiple descriptions. If there is a description that includes multiple instances of both enumeration and chronology separated by a '/', treat each as a separate description and concatenate each in the appropriate field of the output using a ', ' as a delimiter.  
12. The dataset is multilingual, so prefixes and suffixes may vary. Remember the transcription rules outlines above.  
13. Your response must take the form of a valid Python dictionary with no additional text allowed. The basic template to follow is this: ```python {'Enum A':'','Enum B':'','Enum C':'','Enum D':'','Chron I':'','Chron J':'','Chron K':''}```
